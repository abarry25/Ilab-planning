Part one

The full picture

The shape of the job, the portfolio it carries, the rhythm it runs on, and the figures on record as of mid-September 2026.

1.1What this role actually is

Across two years of records, the recurring pattern is converting a program or service into a repeatable operating system — a lifecycle, an ownership model, a calendar, a data flow, an SOP, a feedback loop. Nearly every artifact in the record is infrastructure rather than a one-off.

The clearest articulation of the division of labour comes from the PIC section of the transition document, and it generalises across the portfolio:

Division of labour, as stated in the transition document

Tom owns strategic oversight, venture-development point of view, and judge/network relationships. Becca contributes evaluation philosophy and ecosystem strategy. Advisors own substantive evaluation and founder preparation. This role owns the operating machine.

Three things this implies:

  • The role is the connective tissue between Tech, Marketing, Operations and Advising. Almost nothing it owns can be completed without one of those four teams. Much of the documentation is routing tables and RACI splits rather than content.
  • It is also the institutional memory function. Program debriefs, gap maps, and post-mortems were built to move knowledge out of individual heads.
  • Equity of access is an explicit design goal, not a side effect. The Founder Wayfinder exists because a founder's start at the i-lab correlated with their ability to navigate an institution rather than the merit of their idea. That framing recurs throughout the work.

Judge operations

Tom's notes on the 2026 cycle describe a sequence that runs in order:

  1. Clear last year's judging assignments — either delete the accounts or strip the old information for returning judges. Tanu owned checking this. Skipping it corrupts the new cycle's assignments.
  2. Expectation-setting email from Mailchimp, sent by Tom after RSVP confirmation: rubric, how to evaluate, the evaluation deadline, when applications appear — and an explicit warning that the next email comes from SMA at a different address.
  3. Upload confirmed judges (exported from Salesforce) into SMA with notify via email selected.
  4. Notify when applications go live in judge accounts, from SMA.
  5. Monitor who has not logged in and nudge them — this is manual and is where no-shows are prevented.
  6. Deadline reminder, then individual extension offers and scoring-exception follow-ups for incomplete or outlying reviews.

The full judge comms arc is specified as thirteen sends: three RSVP reminders, orientation, platform-access readiness, pre-launch follow-up, judging launch, mid-cycle reminder, final deadline reminder, post-deadline extension offer, scoring exception handling, judging close and appreciation, feedback collection, finalist announcement, and event re-engagement.

Open technical questions carried to Tanu

Student comms, 2025–26 cycle

The comms matrix is organised into five phases — R2, pre-announcement, post-announcement, after media day, before final judging, post-ceremony — with a named sender, platform and dependency on every row. Structural notes:

  • Senders are split deliberately. SMA-automated for anything tied to application state; Mailchimp for scheduled broadcast; Outlook or Slack for personal and logistical messages; personal “stealth mode” email for attendance chasing.

2.6Learning products — Feedback Lab, Sprints, Primers

Co-led with Rebekah Emanuel. Three products share one architecture: teach first, ask last, with an editable content layer kept separate from the delivery logic.

Feedback Lab

A learning map that converts PIC semifinalist judge feedback into an action step. Formatted feedback from ~9 judges to all semifinalists, both constructive and strength-based, summarised into macro themes with time-bound actions, applicable i-lab events, and on-demand resources.

Journey: receive written feedback → view main themes and identify your own area of need → review actions, activities and resources → engage.

The 2026 synthesis found judge feedback collapsed into four themes across all three tracks, despite track-level differences in emphasis (Open is most sensitive to business-model clarity and traction; Social Impact to sustainability and measurable impact; HCLS to validation rigour and regulatory realism):

ThemeWhat judges are testingSignal phrases in feedback
Customer validationIs the problem real and urgent for a clearly defined audiencebroad target market, nice-to-have, more validation, unmet need, problem feels conceptual
Business model clarityWho pays, why, and whether value becomes durable revenueunclear payer, monetization, pricing not validated, under-developed revenue model
Traction & proofWhether meaningful risk has been reducedpre-traction, conceptual, execution risk, real-world testing, more data
DifferentiationWhy this venture wins in a crowded spacecrowded space, moat, why this team, what’s the wedge, defensible, why now

Each theme carries a routing table of actions, i-lab activities and on-demand resources — the same lookup-table shape used in the Wayfinder.

How sprint access is gated

Sprint access is gated on the workshop. Participants must scan a QR code live in the session and subscribe via the landing page; subscription applies a Mailchimp tag, and that tag triggers the automation. If they do not subscribe before leaving the room, they do not get the sprint. The subscription link must be embedded in the workshop slide deck, along with the pre-survey.

Delivery stack: Eventbrite for the kickoff reminder; Mailchimp for three content emails and three reminders plus a survey chase one week after; Qualtrics for pre- and post-surveys; Airtable for artifact collection; Vimeo for content converted out of Loom. Content emails were last updated 10 June 2026.

Customer Validation Primer — async drip

A fully async alternative for pre-traction ventures with unclear customer identity: ten emails on a two-day cadence, each one a short video plus a 3–5 minute activity with an Airtable form to submit the artifact. Founder Wayfinder / Program Finder

Built solo over summer 2026. The design question was deliberately not “what quiz should we build” but what are the actual questions we ask a founder when we're trying to figure out where to point them?

Five inputs — stage, goal, time commitment, sector, format preference. Stage, goal and time combine through a scoring model to produce a persona; sector modifies what appears within a persona but never changes the persona itself. Two personas are hard overrides rather than scored: Social Impact sector always routes to Impact Founder; light time commitment always routes to Community First.

PersonaWho they are
ExplorerHas an idea, figuring out if it’s real
ValidatorTesting a specific problem with real customers
Sprint BuilderReady to commit, wants intensity and structure
Growth FounderHas traction, focused on fundraising and acceleration
Impact FounderBuilding for social or environmental change
Community FirstLimited bandwidth, not ready for a structured program

Results are three layers: three ordered steps (a persona × sector × step-number lookup, where sector-specific rows override a default fallback, which overrides universal rows); up to four current-semester events filtered by persona and sector tags and sorted by format preference; and office hours filtered by persona.

Maintenance model

  • Two files. founder_xp_generator.html holds the quiz, scoring and renderer and changes only if the program model changes. ilab-data.js holds all content and is the only file touched each semester.
  • Every signup URL lives in one named table at the top of the data file. A semester update is one line, one value.
  • Events and office hours carry active: false to hide without deleting, preserving history.
  • Format mismatches are flagged, not hidden — hiding would create gaps in recommendations.
  • No program directory, by decision. Maximum four events, three steps, a handful of office hours. A starting point, not a catalogue.
  • Supporting infrastructure: a four-tab Google Sheet (Links, Events, Office Hours, Steps) as the human editing interface, a README documenting every column and valid value for a non-technical editor, and a standalone decision-logic page documenting the routing.

3.2The comms engine

The constraints below are taken from the Mailchimp SOP, last reviewed August 2026.

Personalisation fields: First Name, Last Name, Title, Organization, Venture Name. Per-person link fields: Expert URL, Judge Link — these let one send work for everyone who gets it.

Five constraints, stated in the SOP

  1. Never use a contact tag in a conditional block. Conditional merge tags read fields and groups only. A tag holds no value, so it cannot be printed.
  2. Never put conditional logic in a subject line. It is not supported — and this is the single deciding factor for whether something is one email or separate sends.
  3. Never segment or personalise on an unsynced field. It is empty for every contact, so the segment selects nobody and fails silently. Currently empty: Feedback - Improvement, Feedback - Strengths, Ingenuity Award Idea, Sign up, lastname.
  4. Never reference lastname — it duplicates Last Name, is unmapped, and is flagged for deletion.
  5. Never assume a picklist value. Confirm the exact string in Salesforce first: Founder and Student Founder are different audiences.

The word “tag” means three unrelated things in Mailchimp — a merge field's placeholder, a contact label, and (loosely) a segment. The SOP exists mostly to keep those apart. Segments re-run as people change; tags must be reapplied by hand and go stale. Use a tag only where the distinction exists nowhere in Salesforce, and record why.

Seven field requests blocking planned work

Weekly Digest

The test it is designed against: if the Weekly Digest were the only thing a member engaged with, they would still know exactly what to do and when, to get the most out of their membership.

  • Actionable — every item is one click from sign up, register, indicate interest, learn more, or access on demand. If it is not actionable, it goes in the newsletter instead.
  • Brief — maximum one to two scrolls; two to three sentences per paragraph.
  • Time-bound — Mondays, with the covered date range stated. Evergreens are the exception.
  • Relevant — dynamic to member profile, not a static calendar dump.

Structure: optional announcements → blocks and highlights → evergreens → footer. Reference point is an Inshorts or NYT-digest style: short bullets up top, depth below. Nothing two or more weeks out unless it is actionable. First two issues built for 21 and 28 September, 8am.

3.3Customer service, triage, and the email agent

Manual flow: receive → tag → acknowledge → clarify internally → route or respond → archive. Three tech-escalation tiers: Tech 1 POPS can solve it; Tech 2 needs clarification with Tech; Tech 3 backend escalation.

The triage map

The whiteboard map has two layers. An identity gate first — Harvard student, alumnus, product vendor, expert or VC, partnership inquiry — then an intent layer beneath it: how do I get started (route to Wayfinder), alumni resources (route to alumni email), questions about a specific offering, membership issues (kicked out of the application, can't find the application, how to update a profile, how to add team members), and an “other” email form catch-all. Vendors get a decline template; experts and VCs and partnership inquiries both route to the expert intake form.

The email agent build

Copilot Studio workflows against the shared mailbox, specced for Jill, Tom, Viv and Becca. The pain point is precise: identity is looked up by hand in Salesforce for nearly every message; canned language is scattered across saved replies and personal docs so the same question gets three different answers; and program details are hand-copied into whichever template mentions them, so a changed deadline has to be hunted across a dozen files.

The two decisions that determine whether it is trustworthy

Identity. Self-report is acceptable for routing tone and content, but a Salesforce match must be required before the bot ever confirms membership status or unlocks anything member-restricted. The bot should never assert someone's status back to them — only staff-facing tools see the confirmed answer.

Answer source. Start with a curated FAQ, maintained the same way ilab-data.js is: one owner, semester-swappable, versioned. Live website scraping inherits every typo and outdated date already live. Revisit scraping later only for genuinely static reference pages.

Architecture: inbound email → classify who and why → three routes. Known identity with a simple ask gets a template reply; a program question gets an AI draft grounded strictly in the curated FAQ; a membership issue or anything unclear goes to the staff inbox with no bot reply. Every AI-touched reply passes a human before sending through Phases 1 and 2 — a badly drafted email is worse than a slow one.
